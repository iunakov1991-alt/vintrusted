# ENGINE RELIABILITY

1.5T:

- Strong efficiency

- Known oil dilution risk in cold-climate, short-trip usage

2.0T:

- Based on performance-oriented architecture

- Good long-term durability when serviced on time

# TRANSMISSION RELIABILITY

CVT:

- Smooth operation but sensitive to fluid change intervals

- Overheating risk if abused or neglected

10-speed automatic:

- Generally robust

- Occasional shift quality complaints in early builds

Estimated annual maintenance: $650–900

- Turbo-related components (wastegate, sensors),

- AC condenser,

- Brake calipers and suspension bushings.

# ACCIDENT BEHAVIOR

Front-end impacts:

- Potential hidden damage to lower radiator support, condenser, and subframe.

Side impacts:

- Pay attention to alignment, wind noise, and door seal condition.

# MARKET VALUE (CALIFORNIA EXAMPLE)

Typical private-party range:

- $16,400–22,000 depending on trim, mileage, and accident history.

Clean history + low miles command a premium.

Salvage/rebuilt titles heavily discount value and limit financing options.

# INSPECTION CHECKLIST

- Check dipstick for fuel smell in 1.5T engines.

- Scan for stored Honda Sensing / ADAS fault codes.

- Inspect AC performance and condenser condition.

- Test-drive at highway speeds for CVT/10AT behavior and NVH.

<!-- MUST: actionable, concrete checks -->

# TITLE FLAGS

Avoid:

- 3+ prior owners in short time span.

- Fleet / rental history unless heavily discounted.

- Any record of airbag deployment without high-quality repair documentation.

<!-- MUST: link title history to buyer risk -->

# WHO SHOULD BUY

- Commuters needing comfortable, efficient highway car.

- Families wanting active safety and long-term reliability with 2.0T.

# WHO SHOULD AVOID

- Short-trip-only city drivers with 1.5T.

- Buyers expecting sports-car dynamics without maintenance investment.

# FAQ

Q: Is the 1.5T safe to buy?

A: Yes, if used mainly for mixed or highway driving and serviced on time.
